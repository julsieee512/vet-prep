## 1. Anatomical Terminology & Planes

### Directional Terms 🔴
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Cranial** | Toward the head |
| **Caudal** | Toward the tail |
| **Rostral** | Toward the nose (used only on the head) |
| **Dorsal** | Toward the back (top in standing animal) |
| **Ventral** | Toward the belly |
| **Medial** | Toward the midline |
| **Lateral** | Away from the midline |
| **Proximal** | Closer to the body (limbs) |
| **Distal** | Farther from the body (limbs) |
| **Palmar** | Caudal surface of forelimb distal to carpus |
| **Plantar** | Caudal surface of hindlimb distal to tarsus |
| **Superficial / Deep** | Closer to / farther from the surface |

### Planes 🔴
- **Median (mid-sagittal)**: divides body into equal left/right halves
- **Sagittal (parasagittal)**: parallel to median plane, unequal halves
- **Transverse**: perpendicular to long axis (cross-section)
- **Dorsal (frontal)**: divides body into dorsal/ventral parts

## 2. Skeletal System

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Dog skeleton](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/dog-skeleton-fb8e7afba65b439d86d404e37d6a0cc2) · [Horse skeleton](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/horse-skeleton-8d6d0e74398b4591a90bcc447139e395) (Sketchfab)

### Bone Classification 🟡
- **Long bones**: femur, humerus, metacarpals — endochondral ossification, growth plates (physes)
- **Short bones**: carpals, tarsals
- **Flat bones**: skull, scapula, ribs — intramembranous ossification (skull)
- **Irregular bones**: vertebrae
- **Sesamoid bones**: patella, proximal sesamoids of fetlock — develop within tendons

### Bone Growth Plates (Physes) 🔴
- **Growth occurs at physis** (cartilage between epiphysis and metaphysis)
- Closes at species/breed-dependent ages
- **Salter-Harris classification** of physeal fractures (Type I–V):
  - Type I: through physis only
  - Type II: through physis + metaphysis (most common)
  - Type III: through physis + epiphysis (joint involvement)
  - Type IV: through metaphysis + physis + epiphysis
  - Type V: crush injury to physis (worst prognosis)

![Salter-Harris fracture types I-V](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/10/Salter_Harris_Fracture_Types.svg)

*Salter-Harris types I–V. Mnemonic: **SALTR** — **S**lipped (I), **A**bove (II), **L**ower (III), **T**hrough (IV), **R**ammed/crushed (V). (Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0)*

### Skull Anatomy 🟡

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Dog skull](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/dog-skull-7b00f694ce304683b4349e25513ddaab) · [Horse skull](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/photogrametry-horse-skull-63dc75ab2d33487bb624a0f8b2eb1234) (Sketchfab)

- **Bones of cranium**: frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital, sphenoid, ethmoid
- **Bones of face**: maxilla, mandible, nasal, lacrimal, zygomatic, palatine, vomer, incisive
- **Foramina** 🟡:
  - Foramen magnum: spinal cord exit
  - Optic canal: CN II
  - Orbital fissure: CN III, IV, V1, VI
  - Foramen rotundum: CN V2 (maxillary)
  - Foramen ovale: CN V3 (mandibular) — *easy mnemonic: V3 through ovale*
  - 🔴 ⚡ **Infraorbital foramen**: exit of the **infraorbital nerve (CN V2 branch)** onto the face; palpable dorsal to the upper P3/P4 roots — **infraorbital nerve block** anesthetizes the upper lip, nose, incisors, canines, and rostral premolars (dental extractions, laceration repair). Rostral maxillary dental disease (tooth root abscess of P4/M1) often breaks out through this foramen as a **facial swelling/draining tract below the eye**.
  - 🔴 ⚡ **Supraorbital foramen (frontal foramen)**: exit of the **supraorbital/frontal nerve (CN V1 branch)** onto the forehead — **supraorbital nerve block** desensitizes the medial upper eyelid for entropion surgery, eyelid laceration repair, and standing enucleation in horses; also the landmark for the **auriculopalpebral nerve block** (CN VII motor — akinesia of the orbicularis oculi so the horse stops squeezing its eye shut during ophthalmic exam).
  - 🔴 ⚡ **Mandibular foramen** (medial surface of the mandibular ramus) → **mandibular canal** → **mental foramina** (rostral exits on the lateral mandible near P2–P3); the **inferior alveolar nerve (CN V3 branch)** runs through the canal. **Inferior alveolar (mandibular) nerve block** from inside the mouth = regional anesthesia of the entire hemimandible (all lower teeth + lower lip) — used for mandibular extractions and fracture repair. **Mental nerve block** at the mental foramen anesthetizes the rostral lower lip and incisors.
- **Paranasal sinuses**: frontal, maxillary, sphenoidal — clinically relevant for sinusitis/dehorning in cattle

### Limbs — Overview 🟡

#### Forelimb (general)
- **Scapula → humerus → radius/ulna → carpus → metacarpals → phalanges**
- 🔴 **No bony articulation between forelimb and trunk** — held by **synsarcosis** (muscle sling: serratus ventralis, pectorals, rhomboideus). Clinical: a slipped or torn serratus → "dropped shoulder."
  - **Clavicle status by species**: **horse, cow, sheep, goat, pig** = completely absent. **Dog** = small rudimentary clavicle embedded within the brachiocephalicus muscle (may be invisible on rads). **Cat** = small free-floating vestigial clavicle, **visible on thoracic radiographs** (don't mistake for fracture/foreign body). In all species the clavicle does NOT articulate with sternum or scapula, so the synsarcosis still does all the load-bearing work.
- 🔴 Dog/cat: **5 digits** forelimb (digit I = dewclaw, may be missing in some breeds)
- 🔴 Horse: only **digit III** weight-bearing; splint bones (MC II + IV) are vestigial
- 🔴 Cattle/sheep/pig: cloven hoof = digits **III + IV** weight-bearing (II/V = dewclaws in pigs)

#### Hindlimb (general)
- **Pelvis (ilium + ischium + pubis) → femur → tibia/fibula → tarsus → metatarsals → phalanges**
- 🟡 **Calcaneus** (fibular tarsal bone): caudal projection — common calcanean tendon ("Achilles") insertion
- 🟡 **Talus** (tibial tarsal): articulates with tibia at the tarsocrural joint
- 🔴 **Fibula** is reduced in horse (proximal portion only) and absent in ruminants (only proximal head)

### Equine Distal Limb — Detailed Anatomy 🔴

The horse distal limb is one of the **highest-yield NAVLE topics** because of lameness and fracture work. The horse evolved from a 5-digit ancestor to a single weight-bearing digit (digit III) — the "splint bones" (MC II and IV) are vestigial.

#### Bones of the Equine Forelimb (proximal → distal) 🔴
| Bone | Common name | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Scapula** | Shoulder blade | Articulates with humerus; spine of scapula palpable |
| **Humerus** | Arm bone | Greater tubercle palpable cranially (point of shoulder) |
| **Radius** | Forearm | Radius is the major weight-bearing forearm bone in the horse; **fused to ulna distally** |
| **Ulna** | — | Reduced; only proximal portion (olecranon) is significant; fused to radius |
| **Carpal bones** | "Knee" (kniegewricht) | 7–8 bones in 2 rows: proximal (radial, intermediate, ulnar, accessory) + distal (C1–C4); **C1 often absent** |
| **MC III** | "Cannon bone" | Single major weight-bearing metacarpal |
| **MC II + IV** | "Splint bones" | Vestigial, run alongside MC III; **splints** = periostitis of these bones, common training injury |
| **Proximal sesamoid bones** (×2) | — | At palmar fetlock; embedded in suspensory ligament; common fracture site in racehorses |
| **Proximal phalanx (P1)** | Long pastern | Articulates with MC III at fetlock |
| **Middle phalanx (P2)** | Short pastern | Largely within hoof |
| **Distal phalanx (P3)** | Coffin bone / pedal bone | Entirely within hoof; **rotation of P3 = laminitis hallmark** |
| **Distal sesamoid bone** | Navicular bone | Inside hoof, palmar to P2/P3 articulation; **navicular disease** = caudal heel pain |

🔴 **Joints of the equine distal limb (top → bottom):**
- **Fetlock joint** = MC III–P1 = **metacarpophalangeal joint**
- **Pastern joint** = P1–P2 = **proximal interphalangeal joint (PIP)**
- **Coffin joint** = P2–P3 = **distal interphalangeal joint (DIP)** — encloses the navicular bone

🔴 **Bones of the equine hindlimb mirror the forelimb distal to the carpus/tarsus** (MT III + splint MT II/IV, P1, P2, P3, sesamoids, navicular). Differences: tarsus has 6–7 bones; calcaneus has the prominent **tuber calcanei** (point of hock).

#### Critical equine fracture & lameness sites 🔴
- **Splint bones (MC/MT II, IV)**: "splints" = inflammation/exostosis from training stress
- **Proximal sesamoids**: apical fractures in racehorses
- **P1**: spiral and longitudinal fractures
- **P3**: pedal osteitis, fractures, **rotation in laminitis**
- **Navicular bone**: navicular syndrome — chronic forelimb lameness; relieved by palmar digital nerve block
- **Carpus**: chip and slab fractures of carpal bones (especially C3) in racehorses

### Equine Hoof Anatomy 🔴

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Equine hoof anatomy](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/equine-hoof-anatomy-02131a00f40f4241a7693a70c0ddd925) · [Horse hoof section](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/horse-hoof-section-3d9409d17e604ebc9f354c9339ba84dd) (Sketchfab)

**Internal structures (within the hoof capsule):**
- **P2 (distal portion), P3 (entirely), navicular bone**
- **Distal interphalangeal joint (coffin joint)** with **navicular bursa**
- **Deep digital flexor tendon (DDFT)**: passes over the navicular bone (acts as pulley) → inserts on **flexor surface of P3**
- **Common (long) digital extensor tendon**: inserts on **extensor process of P3**
- **Lateral cartilages of P3**: extend dorsally, ossify in older horses ("sidebones")
- **Digital cushion**: fibroelastic shock absorber under P3, between heels

**Hoof wall layers (outside → inside):**
1. **Stratum externum** (periople): glossy outer thin layer
2. **Stratum medium**: pigmented horn — main wall
3. **Stratum internum**: epidermal (insensitive) **laminae** — interlock with **dermal (sensitive) laminae** of P3
   - 🔴 **Laminitis** = inflammation of the lamellar interface → loss of P3 attachment → P3 rotation/sinking

**Sole / frog:**
- **Frog**: V-shaped, elastic, central; concussion absorption; contains **central sulcus**
- **Sole**: concave; **white line** = junction of wall and sole (key landmark for nailing horseshoes)

🔴 **Lameness exam basics**: hoof tester percussion → pain over toe = pedal osteitis/laminitis; over frog = navicular; over heel quarter = abscess.

### Equine Distal Limb Nerves & Diagnostic Nerve Blocks 🔴

![Innervation of the equine foot](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/41/Innervation_pied.PNG)

*Innervation of the equine foot showing the palmar digital nerves and their branches — used to localize lameness via sequential nerve blocks. (Bigomar via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.5)*

**Sensory nerve supply to the equine distal forelimb:**
- **Median + ulnar nerves** → **medial + lateral palmar nerves** (run down the limb on each side of the flexor tendons)
- At the proximal sesamoids, palmar nerves split into:
  - **Palmar digital nerves (medial + lateral)** — sensory to the heel, frog, sole, P3, navicular
  - **Dorsal branches** — sensory to the dorsal coronary band and laminae

**Diagnostic perineural nerve blocks (proximal → distal — used to localize lameness):**
| Block | Site | Desensitizes |
|---|---|---|
| **Palmar digital nerve block (PDNB)** | Just proximal to ungular cartilages, palmaroaxial to digital vessels | Palmar 1/3 of foot — **navicular**, deep DDFT, palmar P3 |
| **Abaxial sesamoid block** | Over abaxial surface of proximal sesamoids | Whole foot + most of pastern |
| **Low palmar (low 4-point) block** | Just proximal to fetlock — both palmar nn. + palmar metacarpal nn. | Fetlock + foot |
| **High palmar (high 4-point) block** | Just distal to carpus | Entire metacarpal region + fetlock + foot |

🔴 The **palmar digital nerve block** is the single most important diagnostic block in equine lameness — if lameness improves, it's foot/heel pain (navicular, P3, deep flexor injury).

### Bovine Digit — Clinical Anatomy 🔴

- Each claw has its own P3 within a horn capsule; **lateral hindlimb claw** and **medial forelimb claw** bear most weight (most lameness)
- **White line** = junction of wall and sole; weak point — white line disease, abscesses
- 🔴 ⚡ **Sole ulcer (Rusterholz ulcer)** — at sole-bulb junction of **lateral hind claw** (typical site); from chronic laminar damage
- 🔴 **P3 osteomyelitis** = severe consequence of deep sepsis → **digit amputation** (claw amputation through P1) often needed
- 🟡 **Footrot (interdigital phlegmon)**: *Fusobacterium necrophorum*; both claws affected, swelling above coronet
- 🟡 **Digital dermatitis (hairy heel warts)**: *Treponema* spp.; bulb of heel; foot bath control

### Brachial Plexus & Lumbosacral Plexus Quick Reference 🟡

**Brachial plexus** (forelimb innervation, C6–T2):
| Nerve | Roots | Major innervation | If injured |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Suprascapular** | C6–C7 | Supraspinatus + infraspinatus | "Sweeney" — atrophy of these muscles, dropped shoulder (horse) |
| **Subscapular + axillary** | C6–C7 | Shoulder flexors | Shoulder instability |
| **Musculocutaneous** | C6–C8 | Biceps brachii (elbow flexor) | Loss of elbow flexion |
| **Radial** | C7–T1(2) | All extensors of elbow, carpus, digits | **Knuckling, dropped elbow, no extension** — most clinically devastating |
| **Median + ulnar** | C8–T1 | Carpal/digital flexors; sensory to palmar surface | Loss of flexion + palmar sensation |

**Lumbosacral plexus** (hindlimb, L4–S2):
| Nerve | Roots | Major innervation | If injured |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Femoral** | L4–L6 | Quadriceps (stifle extension) | Cannot bear weight; collapsing hindlimb |
| **Obturator** | L4–L6 | Adductors | "Splay-leg" in cows after dystocia |
| **Sciatic** | L6–S2 | Most caudal thigh, all distal hindlimb | Knuckling, dropped hock, no flexion |
| → **Tibial branch** | | Caudal crus, plantar flexion | Hyperflexion of hock |
| → **Peroneal (fibular) branch** | | Cranial crus, dorsiflexion | Knuckling, "dropped foot" |

🔴 **Downer cow syndrome**: prolonged recumbency → compression of **sciatic + obturator** nerves → permanent paresis. **Most preventable with early lifting/rolling.**

### Neurolocalization Regions 🔴 (single highest-yield neurology concept)

The spinal cord is divided into 5 functional regions. Identifying the region from physical findings is the **single most important neurology skill on the NAVLE**. UMN vs LMN signs in each limb tell you the region.

| Region | Forelimbs | Hindlimbs | Common causes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Intracranial (forebrain/brainstem/cerebellum)** | Normal or contralateral deficits | Normal or contralateral deficits | Tumor, stroke, GME, hydrocephalus, metabolic |
| 🔴 **C1–C5** | UMN (spastic, ↑ reflexes) | UMN (spastic, ↑ reflexes) | IVDD (cervical), atlanto-axial subluxation, Wobbler |
| 🔴 **C6–T2** (cervical intumescence — brachial plexus) | **LMN** (flaccid, ↓ reflexes, atrophy) | UMN (spastic, ↑ reflexes) | Cervical IVDD, FCE, neoplasia, brachial plexus avulsion |
| 🔴 **T3–L3** | Normal | UMN (spastic, ↑ reflexes) — **classic IVDD presentation** | ⚡ **IVDD Hansen Type I** (chondrodystrophic — Dachshund), FCE, neoplasia |
| 🔴 **L4–S3** (lumbar intumescence — lumbosacral plexus) | Normal | **LMN** (flaccid, ↓ reflexes, atrophy) | Lumbosacral disease, cauda equina, FCE, neoplasia |

🔴 **UMN (upper motor neuron) signs** = lesion ABOVE the limb's spinal segment → spastic paresis, ↑/normal reflexes, late atrophy (disuse only)
🔴 **LMN (lower motor neuron) signs** = lesion AT the limb's spinal segment or peripheral nerve → flaccid paresis, ↓/absent reflexes, **early neurogenic atrophy**, ↓ tone

⚡ **Classic NAVLE pattern**: Dachshund acutely paraplegic, hindlimb hyperreflexia, normal forelimbs → **T3–L3 myelopathy = Hansen Type I IVDD**

#### Horner's Syndrome 🔴
- 🔴 ⚡ Loss of sympathetic innervation to the eye → **MIPP**: **M**iosis (constricted pupil), **I**ridocyclitis-free, **P**tosis (drooping upper eyelid), **P**rotrusion of third eyelid + enophthalmos
- 🔴 Sympathetic pathway: hypothalamus → cervical spinal cord (T1–T3) → cervical sympathetic trunk (in vagosympathetic trunk in cervical region) → cranial cervical ganglion → eye
- 🟡 Localization by lesion site:
  - **First-order (central)**: brainstem, cervical cord
  - **Second-order (preganglionic)**: T1–T3 ventral roots, **brachial plexus injury**, mediastinal mass
  - **Third-order (postganglionic)**: middle ear disease (most common in dog), cranial cervical ganglion
- 🟡 **Pharmacological localization**: 1% phenylephrine — third-order pupil dilates rapidly (denervation hypersensitivity), pre-ganglionic slower

#### Dermatomes 🟡 (sensory testing for nerve root localization)
- **Radial n.** → lateral elbow, dorsal forelimb
- **Ulnar n.** → caudal forelimb, lateral 5th digit
- **Median n.** → palmar forelimb
- **Femoral/saphenous n.** → medial thigh, medial crus
- **Sciatic n.** → caudal thigh, lateral crus
- **Peroneal (fibular) n.** → dorsal hindfoot
- **Tibial n.** → plantar hindfoot

### Joints 🟡
- **Synarthroses (fibrous)**: skull sutures — minimal movement
- **Amphiarthroses (cartilaginous)**: intervertebral discs, mandibular symphysis
- **Diarthroses (synovial)**: most movable joints
  - Components: articular cartilage, synovial membrane, synovial fluid (hyaluronic acid), joint capsule, ligaments

### Common Clinical Anatomy 🔴
- **Hip dysplasia**: shallow acetabulum + femoral head incongruity → osteoarthritis
- **Cranial cruciate ligament (CCL/CrCL)**: prevents cranial drawer of tibia; ruptured commonly in dogs
- **Patella**: small breeds (medial luxation), large breeds (lateral)
- **Atlanto-axial subluxation**: small breed dogs, dens hypoplasia
- **Wobbler syndrome (CVSM)**: cervical vertebral malformation — Doberman, Great Dane, Mastiff

## 3. Muscular System

### Muscle Types 🔴
| Type | Control | Striation | Nuclei | Regeneration | Locations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Skeletal** | Voluntary | Yes | Multiple, peripheral | Yes (satellite cells) | Attached to bone |
| **Cardiac** | Involuntary | Yes | Single, central; intercalated discs | **No** (scar formation) | Heart only |
| **Smooth** | Involuntary | No | Single, central | Yes | GI, vessels, uterus, airways, bladder |

🔴 **Cardiac muscle does not regenerate** — myocardial infarction → fibrous scar → reduced function. This is why heart disease is generally progressive.

### Skeletal Muscle Microanatomy 🟡
- **Muscle fiber (myofiber)** = single multinucleated cell, surrounded by **endomysium**
- **Fascicle** = bundle of fibers, surrounded by **perimysium**
- **Whole muscle** surrounded by **epimysium** → continuous with tendon
- **Sarcomere** = contractile unit between Z-discs (actin + myosin)
- **T-tubules + sarcoplasmic reticulum** release Ca²⁺ → triggers contraction
- **Satellite cells** = muscle stem cells under basal lamina; activated for repair after injury
- 🟡 **Type I fibers**: slow-twitch, oxidative, fatigue-resistant (postural muscles, e.g., soleus)
- 🟡 **Type IIa**: fast-twitch oxidative; **Type IIb/IIx**: fast-twitch glycolytic (sprinting)
- 🟢 Quarter Horses are bred for high Type II proportion → power but predisposed to **PSSM**

### Major Muscle Groups by Region 🟡

#### Cervical / Trunk
- **Brachiocephalicus** — major neck muscle; landmark for jugular venipuncture (vein lies in jugular groove between brachiocephalicus dorsally and sternocephalicus ventrally)
- **Trapezius** (cervical + thoracic parts) — elevates limb
- **Latissimus dorsi** — flexes shoulder; common surgical landmark for thoracotomy
- **Longissimus dorsi** — largest epaxial muscle; "loin"; high-yield in equine **tying-up**
- **Iliocostalis, spinalis** — epaxial group, support spine
- **External + internal abdominal obliques, transversus abdominis, rectus abdominis** — abdominal wall (5 layers including skin/external fascia)
- 🟡 **Linea alba** = midline tendinous raphe between left/right rectus abdominis — standard ventral midline laparotomy approach (avascular)

#### Forelimb
| Muscle | Action | Innervation | Clinical relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Supraspinatus** | Shoulder extension | Suprascapular n. | Atrophies in **Sweeney** |
| **Infraspinatus** | Shoulder lateral stabilizer | Suprascapular n. | Atrophies in **Sweeney** |
| **Triceps brachii** | Elbow extension | Radial n. | Loss → "dropped elbow" (radial paralysis) |
| **Biceps brachii** | Elbow flexion, shoulder extension | Musculocutaneous n. | Bicipital bursitis (horse) |
| **Extensor carpi radialis** | Carpal extension | Radial n. | |
| **Deep + superficial digital flexors** | Digit/carpal flexion | Median + ulnar n. | **Bowed tendon** (SDFT) in racehorses |

#### Hindlimb
| Muscle | Action | Innervation | Clinical relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gluteus medius** | Hip extension | Cranial gluteal n. | **Largest muscle in horse**; major IM injection site |
| **Quadriceps** (4 heads) | Stifle extension | Femoral n. | Femoral n. block for stifle surgery |
| **Hamstrings** (biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus) | Hip extension, stifle flexion | Sciatic n. | **Fibrotic myopathy** (semitendinosus, horse) |
| **Gastrocnemius** | Hock extension | Tibial n. | Forms common calcanean tendon → tuber calcanei |
| **Cranial tibial** | Hock flexion | Peroneal n. | Loss → "knuckling" (peroneal paralysis) |

#### Respiratory
- **Diaphragm** — principal inspiratory muscle, dome-shaped, **innervated by phrenic n. (C5–C7)**
- **External intercostals** — inspiration; **Internal intercostals** — expiration
- 🔴 Phrenic nerve damage → diaphragmatic paralysis → respiratory distress

### Intramuscular (IM) Injection Sites — by Species 🔴

This is **high-yield clinical anatomy** — wrong site = nerve damage, abscess, or carcass condemnation.

#### Horse 🔴
![Horse muscles, dorsal/back view](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c0/Muscles_of_a_horse_-_back_view._Wellcome_L0009806.jpg)

*Horse musculature, dorsal view (Wellcome Collection L0009806). Shows the bulk of the gluteal mass and longissimus dorsi — sites used for IM injection in the horse. (Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 4.0)*

| Site | Landmarks | Volume | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lateral cervical (neck)** 🔴 | Triangle: nuchal ligament (dorsal) / cervical vertebrae (ventral) / scapula (caudal) | ≤20 mL | **Most common**; safe, good drainage. Stay in middle 1/3 of neck. |
| **Pectoral (brisket)** | Cleft between pectoralis muscles, cranial to forelimb | ≤10 mL | Allows drainage if abscess; may cause stiffness |
| **Gluteal** | Tuber sacrale to tuber coxae line | ≤15 mL | Poor drainage if abscess → use only when other sites unavailable |
| **Semimembranosus / semitendinosus** | Caudal thigh | ≤15 mL | Avoid sciatic nerve (caudal) |

🔴 **Always aspirate** before injecting (avoid IV injection). 🔴 **Procaine penicillin IV → fatal reaction** (CNS excitement, collapse).

#### Cattle 🔴
- **Lateral neck (BQA-preferred)** 🔴 — same triangle as horse. **Beef Quality Assurance (BQA)** mandates **all injections in front of the shoulder** to protect valuable hindquarter cuts.
- **Subcutaneous over the neck/shoulder** (preferred when label allows: "SQ when possible")
- 🔴 Avoid hindquarters/topline — causes scar/condemnation in beef carcass
- Volume: ≤10 mL per site in adult; ≤5 mL in calves; **split larger doses** across multiple sites

#### Small ruminants (sheep, goat) 🟡
- **Lateral neck**, **triceps** (caudal to scapula), **semimembranosus**
- Volume ≤5 mL per site

#### Pig 🟡
- **Lateral neck behind ear** (preferred — safe, fast)
- **Gluteal** (less common in pigs raised for meat)
- 🟡 In piglets: caudal to ear or in semimembranosus
- 🔴 Iron dextran is given IM in newborn piglets (~200 mg) into caudal thigh

#### Dog & Cat 🟡
- **Epaxial muscles (lumbar)** — lateral to dorsal midline, L3–L5
- **Quadriceps (cranial thigh)** — common in cats; **avoid sciatic** (caudal thigh)
- **Triceps (caudal to humerus)** — small dogs/cats
- 🔴 **Never inject in caudal thigh in cats** — sciatic nerve injury common; **use cranial thigh (quadriceps)** or epaxial
- Volume: ≤2 mL per site in cats, ≤5 mL in medium dogs

#### Bird 🟡
- **Pectoralis major (breast)** — primary site; aim laterally to avoid sternum
- 🟡 Renal portal system controversy: theoretical concern that drugs in caudal half drain to kidneys first (use cranial half)

### Muscle Pathologies — High Yield 🔴

#### Equine Myopathies 🔴

**Exertional rhabdomyolysis (ER) / "Tying-up"** 🔴
- Acute muscle damage during/after exercise → stiff, sweating, reluctant to move
- Affected muscles: **gluteals, longissimus dorsi, semimembranosus** (firm, painful)
- 🔴 **Myoglobinuria** → coffee-colored urine → risk of acute kidney injury
- 🔴 Diagnostics: **CK ↑↑ (peaks 4–6 h)**, **AST ↑ (peaks 24–48 h, slow decline)**
- Treatment: stall rest, IV fluids (protect kidneys), NSAIDs, sedation, dietary modification

**Polysaccharide Storage Myopathy (PSSM)** 🔴
- Genetic; abnormal glycogen accumulation in muscle
- **PSSM Type 1**: GYS1 gene mutation (Quarter Horse, draft, warmblood). Diagnose: **genetic test**
- **PSSM Type 2**: histologic diagnosis (muscle biopsy) — no single gene identified
- Management: **low-starch, high-fat diet** + regular exercise

**Hyperkalemic Periodic Paralysis (HYPP)** 🔴
- 🔴 **Quarter Horses** descended from sire **"Impressive"** (autosomal dominant SCN4A mutation)
- Episodic muscle fasciculations, weakness, third eyelid prolapse, collapse
- Trigger: high-K⁺ feed, fasting, stress
- 🔴 Bloodwork during episode: **hyperkalemia + normal CK** (distinguishes from ER)
- Management: low-K⁺ diet (avoid alfalfa, molasses), acetazolamide

**Equine Motor Neuron Disease (EMND)** 🟡
- Vitamin E deficiency → ventral horn motor neuron degeneration
- Trembling, weight shifting, low head carriage, muscle atrophy
- Diagnose: vitamin E levels low, sacrocaudalis dorsalis medialis biopsy

**Fibrotic Myopathy** 🟡
- Scarring of semitendinosus → mechanical "slap-down" gait at walk
- Result of trauma; treat with myectomy/tenectomy

**White Muscle Disease (Nutritional Myopathy)** 🔴
- 🔴 **Selenium and/or vitamin E deficiency** in foals, calves, lambs
- Bilateral, symmetric muscle degeneration → weakness, recumbency, dysphagia
- Can affect cardiac muscle → **sudden death**
- 🔴 **Whitish pale muscle** on necropsy (calcium-laden)
- Management: Se/Vit E injection; prevent with maternal supplementation

#### Clostridial Myonecrosis 🔴
| Disease | Organism | Trigger | Species |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blackleg** | *C. chauvoei* | Spores activate from latent muscle spores after bruising | Cattle (6mo–2yr), sheep |
| **Malignant edema** | *C. septicum* | Wound contamination | All species |
| **Black disease** | *C. novyi* type B | **Fluke migration** through liver activates spores | Sheep, cattle |
| **Bacillary hemoglobinuria** | *C. haemolyticum* (*C. novyi* type D) | Liver fluke migration | Cattle |

🔴 Blackleg classic exam triad: **young thrifty cattle, sudden death, crepitant swelling** (gas in muscle). Prevent with 7-way / 8-way clostridial vaccine.

#### Small Animal Myopathies 🟡
- **Masticatory Muscle Myositis (MMM)** 🔴 — autoimmune attack on **type 2M fibers** unique to muscles of mastication (temporalis, masseter, pterygoid, digastricus). Acute = swollen jaw, painful opening; Chronic = fibrosis, "trismus" (can't open mouth). Dx: **2M antibody titer**. Tx: immunosuppressive prednisone.
- **Immune-Mediated Polymyositis** 🟡 — generalized weakness, ↑ CK; Newfoundland, Boxer predisposed
- **Labrador Centronuclear Myopathy** 🟢 — congenital, exercise-induced collapse
- **Hypokalemic Myopathy (cat)** 🟡 — low K⁺ → ventroflexion of neck (cervical weakness); causes: CKD, hyperaldosteronism

#### Other Important Conditions 🔴
- **Sweeney (suprascapular n. injury)** 🔴 — collar/harness trauma; atrophy of supraspinatus + infraspinatus → "shoulder slip" (lateral instability when bearing weight)
- **Downer cow syndrome** 🔴 — recumbency >12 h → compression of **gluteal + sciatic + obturator nerves**, plus muscle ischemia → permanent paralysis. Roll cow every 2 h; deep bedding.
- **Malignant Hyperthermia** 🟡 — RYR1 mutation → uncontrolled Ca²⁺ release from SR triggered by **halothane, succinylcholine** → rigidity, hyperthermia, acidosis. **Pigs (PSS), occasionally horses, dogs**. Treat with **dantrolene**.
- **Megaesophagus** (smooth + striated muscle disorder) — see Digestive
- **Myasthenia gravis** 🟡 — autoantibodies vs. ACh receptor at NMJ. Dogs (acquired). Sx: exercise-induced weakness, megaesophagus, regurgitation. Dx: ACh receptor antibody titer.

### Muscle Enzymes — Bloodwork 🔴
| Enzyme | T½ | Peak post-injury | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CK (creatine kinase)** 🔴 | 2–4 h | **4–6 h** | Most sensitive/specific for muscle; rapid clearance |
| **AST (aspartate aminotransferase)** 🔴 | 7–10 d | **24–48 h** | Also liver — interpret with CK |
| **LDH** 🟡 | 1–2 d | 24–48 h | Non-specific (liver, RBC, muscle) |
| **Myoglobin (urine)** 🔴 | minutes | acute | Coffee-colored urine; positive blood on dipstick **without RBCs** on sediment |

🔴 **CK normal + AST elevated** = old muscle injury (>72 h ago) **OR** liver disease.
🔴 **CK + AST both elevated** = acute/ongoing muscle injury.

### Muscle Biopsy Sites 🟡
- **Horse**: semimembranosus or sacrocaudalis dorsalis medialis (for EMND)
- **Dog**: vastus lateralis (quadriceps), triceps
- Always sample **affected** muscle + control if asymmetric

#### Chambers & Flow
```
Body → Cranial/Caudal vena cava → RIGHT ATRIUM → tricuspid valve →
RIGHT VENTRICLE → pulmonary valve → pulmonary artery → LUNGS →
pulmonary veins → LEFT ATRIUM → mitral (bicuspid) valve →
LEFT VENTRICLE → aortic valve → aorta → BODY
```

🔴 **AV valves**: tricuspid (right), mitral/bicuspid (left)
🔴 **Semilunar valves**: pulmonic, aortic
🔴 **Chordae tendineae** anchor AV valve cusps to **papillary muscles**, preventing valve eversion during systole

#### Heart Layers 🟡
- **Endocardium** → **myocardium** → **epicardium (visceral pericardium)** → pericardial space → **parietal pericardium**

#### Conduction System 🔴
**SA node (right atrium) → AV node → Bundle of His → Right & Left bundle branches → Purkinje fibers**

![Cardiac conduction system](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e7/Electrical_conduction_system_of_the_heart.svg)

*Cardiac electrical conduction — SA node, AV node, Bundle of His, bundle branches, Purkinje fibers. **The conduction pathway is identical across all domestic mammals** — only intrinsic rates differ (cat ~120, dog ~70, horse ~35, cow ~60 bpm). (Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0)*

🟡 SA node = pacemaker (~70 bpm dog, ~120 bpm cat at rest)
🟡 AV node delay (~0.1 s) allows atrial contraction before ventricular

### Coronary Circulation 🟡
- **Right and left coronary arteries** arise from aortic sinuses
- Left coronary → **circumflex + paraconal interventricular** branches
- Right coronary → **subsinuosal (right) interventricular** branch

### Major Vessels 🟡

#### Arterial
- **Aorta**: ascending → arch → descending (thoracic → abdominal)
- 🔴 **Aortic arch branching pattern differs by species** (NAVLE-favorite species comparison):
  - **Horse, dog, cat**: 2 branches off arch — **brachiocephalic trunk + left subclavian**
  - **Cattle, pig**: SINGLE **brachiocephalic trunk only** (left subclavian arises from brachiocephalic)
  - **Small ruminants**: similar to cattle
- 🟡 Abdominal aorta branches: celiac, cranial mesenteric, renal, caudal mesenteric, external iliac (hindlimbs)

#### Venous
- **Cranial vena cava**: drains head, neck, forelimbs
- **Caudal vena cava**: drains abdomen, pelvis, hindlimbs
- **Hepatic portal vein**: GI → liver (then through hepatic veins → caudal vena cava)
- 🔴 Drug significance: oral drugs go GI → portal vein → liver (first-pass metabolism)

### Fetal Circulation 🔴

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Fetal circulation shunts](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=fetal+circulation&type=models) · [Ductus arteriosus](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=ductus+arteriosus&type=models) (Sketchfab)

**Three key shunts:**
1. **Ductus venosus**: umbilical vein → caudal vena cava (bypasses liver)
2. **Foramen ovale**: right atrium → left atrium (bypasses pulmonary circ)
3. **Ductus arteriosus**: pulmonary artery → aorta (bypasses lungs)

🔴 ⚡ **Patent ductus arteriosus (PDA)**: failure to close → **continuous "machinery" murmur** (heard left heart base), left-to-right shunt, **L-sided cardiomegaly**; **most common congenital cardiac defect in dogs**; **female small breeds** predisposed (Maltese, Pomeranian); coil/ligation curative

🟡 ⚡ **Persistent right aortic arch (PRAA)**: vascular ring anomaly — esophagus entrapped between aorta, pulmonary artery, and ligamentum arteriosum → **regurgitation at weaning** when solid food introduced; **megaesophagus cranial to heart base** on rads

![Embryonic aortic arches](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/84/Gray473.png)

*Embryology of the aortic arches. Normally the **left 4th arch** becomes the definitive aortic arch. In PRAA the **right 4th arch** persists instead — the aorta passes over the right side of the esophagus, trapping it together with the left-sided ligamentum arteriosum. (Gray's Anatomy, public domain)*

```
Normal:                        PRAA:
       (L)                            (R)
   aortic arch                    aortic arch
        |                              |
   ─────┼─── esophagus            ─────┼─── esophagus
        |   (free)                     X  ← trapped by ring
   pulmonary a.                   pulmonary a. + lig. arteriosum
```

## 5. Lymphatic System

![Lymphatic system overview](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b3/TE-Lymphatic_system_diagram.svg)

*Lymphatic structures — nodes, vessels, thoracic duct, spleen, thymus (human shown). **No equivalent vet diagram exists on free image repositories**, but the general topography (mandibular ↔ submandibular, axillary, inguinal, popliteal, mediastinal, mesenteric) and drainage logic is the same in dogs and cats. Use the table below for vet-specific node names and palpation sites. (Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0)*

### Overview 🔴
- Parallel drainage system to blood vasculature
- Returns interstitial fluid + absorbed fats (chyle from GI) to the venous circulation
- Filters antigens through lymph nodes → immune surveillance
- **One-way flow** — lymphatics have valves but no pump; driven by muscle contraction and respiration

### Lymph Nodes — Palpable Sites in Dog/Cat 🔴

These are the nodes you can palpate on every physical exam. Enlargement suggests regional pathology or systemic lymphoma.

| Node | Location | Drains |
|---|---|---|
| **Mandibular** | Ventral to angle of mandible | Head, rostral face, oral cavity |
| **Superficial cervical (prescapular)** | Cranial to scapula, under trapezius | Head, neck, cranial forelimb |
| **Axillary** | Medial to shoulder, under latissimus | Forelimb, thoracic wall, mammary glands (cranial) |
| **Superficial inguinal** | Inguinal fat pad, medial thigh | Mammary glands (caudal), ventral abdomen, perineum, tail, penis/prepuce |
| **Popliteal** | Caudal aspect of stifle | Distal hindlimb |

🔴 **Generalized lymphadenopathy** (all nodes enlarged) = think **lymphoma** in dogs (most common hematopoietic tumor).
🔴 **Regional lymphadenopathy** = look for infection or tumor in the drainage field of that node.

### Deep / Non-Palpable Nodes 🟡
- **Mediastinal** (cranial thoracic) — draining thoracic wall, heart, esophagus
- **Tracheobronchial** — lungs; enlarged in lymphoma, fungal, or metastatic disease
- **Mesenteric** — intestinal; enlarged in lymphangiectasia, IBD, lymphoma (especially cats)
- **Medial iliac (lumbar aortic)** — pelvic organs, hindlimbs; palpable rectally in large breeds; key node for prostatic and perineal cancer staging
- **Hepatic / splenic / gastric** — upper GI

### Major Lymphatic Vessels 🟡
- **Thoracic duct**: largest lymphatic trunk; starts at the **cisterna chyli** (dorsal to aorta, in abdomen, L1–L3), ascends through diaphragm, empties into the **left external jugular or cranial vena cava** near the thoracic inlet.
  - 🔴 Rupture → **chylothorax** (milky pleural effusion with chylomicrons, high triglycerides).
- **Right lymphatic duct**: drains right forelimb, right side of head/neck, right thorax.

### Spleen 🟡

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Spleen anatomy](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=spleen+anatomy&type=models) (Sketchfab)

- **Red pulp**: sinuses — filters aged RBCs, stores platelets
- **White pulp**: lymphoid follicles (PALS — periarteriolar lymphoid sheaths) — immune function
- Species differences:
  - 🔴 **Dog, horse**: **sinusoidal** spleen — large RBC reservoir; splenic contraction (α-adrenergic) releases RBCs in exercise/excitement → transient rise in PCV
  - 🔴 **Cat, ruminants, humans**: **non-sinusoidal** — less RBC storage
- Clinical: splenic masses are common in older dogs (hemangiosarcoma >> benign hematoma/nodular hyperplasia)

### Thymus 🟢
- Cranial mediastinum, most active in neonates/juveniles
- Site of T-cell maturation (positive + negative selection)
- Involutes after puberty — but a thymic mass in a young cat → suspect **thymoma** or **mediastinal lymphoma** (common in FeLV+ cats)

### Tonsils 🟢
- Palatine tonsils sit in tonsillar crypts, lateral to base of tongue
- Part of **MALT** (mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue) — along with Peyer's patches (ileum) and bronchus-associated lymphoid tissue (BALT)

### Clinical Pearls 🔴
- **Lymph node aspirate** is the single most useful diagnostic for generalized lymphadenopathy — don't aspirate the mandibular node (reactive from oral flora); prefer popliteal or prescapular.
- **Sentinel node mapping** for tumors: know the drainage field of the primary tumor to know which node to biopsy for staging.
- **Metastasis pattern**: most carcinomas (mammary, perianal, prostatic) spread first to regional node → thoracic duct → lungs.

## 6. Respiratory System

### Upper Airway 🟡
**Nostrils → nasal cavity → nasopharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli**

#### Nasal Cavity 🟡
- Conchae/turbinates: dorsal, ventral, ethmoidal — warm/humidify air; site of atrophic rhinitis (pigs — Bordetella + Pasteurella)

#### Larynx 🟡

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Larynx anatomy](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=larynx+anatomy&type=models) · [Equine larynx](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=equine+larynx&type=models) · [Arytenoid cartilage](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=arytenoid+cartilage&type=models) (Sketchfab)

- **Cartilages**: epiglottis, thyroid, cricoid, **arytenoid (paired)**, interarytenoid
- 🔴 ⚡ **Laryngeal paralysis (lar par)**: failure of arytenoid abduction (CN X / recurrent laryngeal); inspiratory **stridor**, exercise intolerance; **older large-breed dogs (Labrador most classic)**, aged horses ("**roaring**" — left-sided 95%); part of **GOLPP** complex in dogs (geriatric onset laryngeal paralysis polyneuropathy); tx = **unilateral arytenoid lateralization (tie-back)**
- 🟡 **Recurrent laryngeal nerve**: branch of vagus; loops around **right subclavian (R)** and **aortic arch (L)** — longer L path → predilection for left-sided disease in horses
- 🟡 ⚡ **Dorsal Displacement of Soft Palate (DDSP)** in horses: long soft palate displaces dorsal to epiglottis during exercise → "**gurgling**" sound, exercise intolerance; tongue-tie / Llewellyn procedure / tie-forward management

#### Guttural Pouches 🔴 (UNIQUE TO HORSE)
- **Bilateral diverticula of the auditory (Eustachian) tubes** — 300-500 mL each
- 🔴 ⚡ **Critical structures coursing through pouch walls**:
  - **CN VII (facial)** — superficial in stylohyoid region
  - **CN IX (glossopharyngeal)**, **CN X (vagus)**, **CN XI (accessory)**, **CN XII (hypoglossal)**
  - **Internal carotid artery** (in dorsal medial compartment) — RUPTURE = fatal epistaxis
  - **Sympathetic trunk + cranial cervical ganglion** → Horner's if affected
- 🔴 ⚡ **Guttural pouch mycosis** (Aspergillus) — erodes internal carotid → **catastrophic epistaxis from one nostril**, often fatal; surgical balloon occlusion of carotid
- 🔴 ⚡ **Guttural pouch empyema** = sequela of strangles (Streptococcus equi), chondroids form

#### Trachea 🔴
- C-shaped (incomplete dorsally) cartilage rings, bridged by **trachealis muscle dorsally**
- 🔴 ⚡ **Tracheal collapse**: dorsal trachealis muscle laxity; **small breed middle-aged dogs (Yorkie classic)**; "**goose-honk**" cough; cervical (inspiratory collapse) vs intrathoracic (expiratory collapse)
- 🟢 Bird airway: trachea bifurcates at **syrinx** (vocal organ — bird-equivalent of larynx)

### Lower Airway & Lungs 🔴

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Lungs anatomy](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=lungs+anatomy&type=models) · [Bronchial tree](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=bronchial+tree&type=models) · [Avian respiratory air sacs](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=bird+air+sacs&type=models) (Sketchfab)

#### Lung Lobes
| Species | Right Lung | Left Lung |
|---|---|---|
| **Dog/Cat** | Cranial, Middle, Caudal, **Accessory** | Cranial (cranial + caudal parts), Caudal |
| **Horse** | Cranial, Caudal, **Accessory** (no middle) | Cranial, Caudal |
| **Cattle** | Cranial (cranial + caudal parts), Middle, Caudal, **Accessory** | Cranial (cranial + caudal parts), Caudal |
| **Pig** | Cranial, Middle, Caudal, Accessory | Cranial (cranial + caudal), Caudal |

🔴 **Right has more lobes than left** in most species (heart sits on left, displaces left lung)
🟡 **Cattle**: right cranial lobe receives a separate **tracheal bronchus** that branches off the trachea before the bifurcation — clinically relevant for bronchopneumonia distribution

#### Pleura 🟡
- **Visceral pleura** (covers lungs) + **parietal pleura** (lines thoracic wall) → potential space = **pleural cavity** with thin film of pleural fluid
- 🔴 Negative intrapleural pressure keeps lungs expanded; loss of seal → pneumothorax → lung collapse
- 🟡 Mediastinum: between left/right pleural cavities; contains heart, great vessels, esophagus, trachea, thymus
  - 🔴 In **horses and ruminants** the mediastinum is COMPLETE (unilateral pneumothorax stays unilateral); in **dogs/cats** it has fenestrations (bilateral pneumothorax common)

### Breathing Mechanics 🟡
- Inspiration: diaphragm contracts (flattens), external intercostals contract → thorax expands → negative pressure → air in
- Expiration: passive at rest (elastic recoil); active during exercise (abdominal muscles, internal intercostals)

### Species-Specific 🟡
- 🔴 **Horses are obligate nasal breathers** — soft palate position; cannot mouth-breathe
- **Birds**: air sacs (cranial + caudal), unidirectional airflow through parabronchi; no diaphragm

- **Mandibular, sublingual, zygomatic, buccal**: mucoid/mixed
- 🟡 **Mucocele** = ranula (sublingual swelling); common in dogs

### Stomach — Monogastric 🔴

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Dog stomach](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=dog+stomach&type=models) · [Stomach anatomy](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=stomach+anatomy&type=models) (Sketchfab)

**Regions**: cardia → fundus → body → pyloric antrum → pylorus
- Cells:
  - **Parietal cells** → HCl + intrinsic factor (B12 absorption)
  - **Chief cells** → pepsinogen → pepsin (acid-activated)

  - **Mucus cells** → protective mucus + bicarbonate

### Stomach — Ruminant 🔴

![Ruminant four-compartment stomach](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/55/Ruminant_digestive_system.png)

*The four ruminant compartments: rumen, reticulum, omasum, abomasum. Food passes reticulum → rumen → omasum → abomasum. (Wikimedia Commons, public domain)*

🔗 **Interactive 3D model**: [Ruminant stomach + omentum majus (Sketchfab)](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/ruminant-stomachomentum-majus-89fe35a64ffa463e8906636bc8b55d39)

**4 compartments (in order of food passage):**
1. **Reticulum** ("honeycomb"): cranial-most; ⚡ site of **hardware disease (traumatic reticuloperitonitis, TRP)** from ingested wire/nails — **withers pinch + bar test positive, anterior abdominal pain**; magnet prevention
2. **Rumen** ("paunch"): largest; on **LEFT side** (left flank ping = LDA exception, but most rumen pings = rumen); pillars create dorsal/ventral sacs; fermentation by microbes (bacteria, protozoa, fungi); ⚡ **rumen cannula site = left paralumbar fossa**
3. **Omasum** ("manyplies"): leaves absorb water; small particles pass through
4. **Abomasum** ("true stomach"): glandular; equivalent to monogastric stomach; **acid digestion**; ⚡ **LDA = left displaced abomasum** (gas-filled abomasum migrates under rumen to L flank → ping); ⚡ **RDA/abomasal volvulus** = right-sided

🔴 **Rumen is on LEFT** — important for surgical approach (left flank for rumenotomy/LDA correction)
🔴 **Rumen pH normally 6.0–7.0**; drops in **SARA** (subacute ruminal acidosis) with high grain diets
🔴 **VFAs produced**: acetate (60%), propionate (20%), butyrate (15%) — primary energy source; propionate is main glucose precursor
🟡 **Eructation**: belching of gas (CO2, methane) — failure → bloat
🟡 **Cud** = regurgitated bolus chewed during rumination; ~8h/day

#### Reticular Groove (Esophageal Groove) 🟡
- Closes during nursing in young ruminants → milk bypasses rumen, goes directly to abomasum
- Function disappears with weaning
- 🟢 Stimulated by suckling and copper sulfate (clinically used to deliver oral medication directly to abomasum)

### Stomach — Equine 🟡

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Horse stomach](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=horse+stomach&type=models) · [Equine GI tract](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=equine+digestive&type=models) (Sketchfab)

- **Single-chamber** but has TWO mucosal regions:
  - **Squamous (non-glandular)** dorsal portion — separated by **margo plicatus** from
  - **Glandular** ventral portion
- 🔴 **Equine gastric ulcer syndrome (EGUS)**:
  - Squamous ulcers = exposure to acid (most common); racehorses, training stress
  - Glandular ulcers = NSAID use, stress
- 🟡 Cannot vomit (strong cardiac sphincter + acute esophageal angle) → gastric rupture if overfilled

![Horse external anatomy](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/21/Horse_anatomy.svg)

*Equine surface anatomy — reference for regional nomenclature used in colic exams, lameness, and field palpation. (Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0)*

### Small Intestine 🟡
- **Duodenum → jejunum (longest) → ileum**
- Length varies by species (very long in herbivores)
- Microscopic: **villi + microvilli** maximize absorption surface
- Crypts of Lieberkühn: stem cells, secretion
- Brunner's glands (duodenum): bicarbonate secretion
- Peyer's patches (ileum): immune (B-cells)

### Large Intestine 🔴

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Horse colon / large intestine](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=horse+colon&type=models) · [Equine cecum](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=equine+cecum&type=models) (Sketchfab) — visualize the pelvic flexure & dorsal/ventral colon

#### Cecum
- 🔴 **Horse**: massive cecum (~30 L) on right side; comma-shaped; site of microbial fermentation (similar to ruminant rumen but post-gastric)
  - Cecum has **base, body, apex** + **ileocecal junction** + **cecocolic junction**
  - **Cecal impaction** = colic emergency
- 🟡 **Cattle**: small cecum
- 🟡 **Dog/cat**: small, vestigial cecum

#### Colon (species-specific anatomy is critical for surgery!)
- 🔴 **Horse colon**: very complex — common site of colic
  - **Right ventral colon → sternal flexure → left ventral colon → pelvic flexure → left dorsal colon → diaphragmatic flexure → right dorsal colon → transverse colon → small (descending) colon**
  - 🔴 **Pelvic flexure**: narrow point; common impaction site
  - 🔴 **Right and left dorsal/ventral colon**: site of large colon volvulus/displacement
- **Ruminant spiral colon**: ascending colon coiled in spiral (centripetal/centrifugal turns) within mesentery
- **Dog colon**: simple — ascending, transverse, descending; short

#### Rectum & Anus
- **Rectum**: terminal portion; lined by mucous membrane
- **Anus**: 2 sphincters — internal (smooth, autonomic) + external (striated, voluntary, pudendal nerve)
- 🟡 **Anal sacs (dogs/cats)**: paired, lateral to anus at **4 and 8 o'clock**; impaction common
  - 🔴 ⚡ **Anal sac adenocarcinoma (apocrine gland adenocarcinoma)** in older dogs (esp. Cocker Spaniel, English Springer) → **paraneoplastic hypercalcemia of malignancy** (PTHrP); also metastasizes to **medial iliac (sublumbar) lymph nodes** — palpate rectally

### Liver 🟡

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Liver anatomy](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=liver+anatomy&type=models) · [Dog liver lobes](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=dog+liver&type=models) (Sketchfab)

- **Lobes**: right (lateral, medial), left (lateral, medial), quadrate, caudate (with caudate process)
- **Gallbladder**: present in dogs, cats, cattle, sheep, goats, pigs — **ABSENT in horses, deer, rats**
- **Hepatic ducts** → **common bile duct** + cystic duct → opens into duodenum at major duodenal papilla
- 🔴 **Hepatic portal vein** drains GI/spleen/pancreas into liver — drugs first pass; toxins detoxified
- 🔴 **Liver functions**: protein synthesis (albumin, clotting factors), bile production, detox, glycogen storage, urea cycle, vitamin storage

### Pancreas 🟡
- **Endocrine** (islets of Langerhans): α-cells (glucagon), β-cells (insulin), δ-cells (somatostatin), F/PP cells (pancreatic polypeptide)
- **Exocrine** (acini): digestive enzymes (amylase, lipase, trypsinogen, chymotrypsinogen)
- 🟡 **Pancreatic ducts**: most species have 2 (main + accessory); empty into duodenum
- 🔴 **Acute pancreatitis** in dogs (esp. Schnauzer, Labrador): cranial abdominal pain, vomiting; predisposed by high-fat meals

## 8. Urinary System

### Kidney Gross Anatomy 🔴

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Kidney anatomy](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=kidney+anatomy&type=models) · [Bovine kidney lobulated](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=bovine+kidney&type=models) · [Nephron](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=nephron&type=models) (Sketchfab)

- **Bean-shaped, retroperitoneal**, paired
- **Right kidney typically more cranial** (especially in dogs/horses); in cattle right is more caudal due to liver
- 🔴 Layers: **cortex** (outer, glomeruli + convoluted tubules) → **medulla** (inner, loops of Henle + collecting ducts) → **renal pelvis** (urine collection) → **ureter**

### Species Variation 🔴
| Species | Kidney shape | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dog/cat/sheep/goat | **Smooth, unipapillary** (single papilla = **renal crest** → renal pelvis) | "Beans" |
| Horse | Smooth unipapillary; ⚡ **Right kidney heart-shaped**, left bean-shaped | Right = harder to palpate per rectum (under last rib) |
| Cattle | **Lobulated** externally, multiple papillae, **NO renal pelvis** — calyces drain to ureter directly | "Bunch of grapes" |
| Pig | **Smooth externally, multilobar internally**, multiple papillae + true renal pelvis | |

🟡 **Renal crest vs renal pelvis** (clinical imaging) — unipapillary kidneys have a **renal crest** (single fused papilla), seen as a triangular structure in the dilated pelvis on US; a normal "anechoic gap" not to mistake for hydronephrosis

### Nephron 🔴 (microscopic functional unit)

```
Renal corpuscle (glomerulus + Bowman's capsule)
        ↓
Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)
        ↓
Loop of Henle (descending → ascending)
        ↓
Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT)
        ↓
Collecting duct → Papilla → Pelvis → Ureter
```

🔴 See Physiology notes for detailed function of each segment.

### Lower Urinary Tract 🟡
- **Ureters**: enter bladder dorsally; oblique passage through bladder wall = anti-reflux mechanism
- **Bladder (vesica urinaria)**: trigone (between ureteral openings + urethral opening)
- **Urethra**:
  - **Female**: short, opens into vestibule (between vagina and vulva)
  - **Male**: longer; passes through pelvic urethra → penile urethra
  - 🔴 **Sigmoid flexure (S-shape) of penis** in ruminants and pigs — site of urolith obstruction in steers/wethers
  - 🔴 **Urethral process** in small ruminants — narrow vermiform appendage at glans; common urolith site (amputate during surgical management)

### Clinical Anatomy 🔴
- **Urethral obstruction** in male cats (FLUTD) — narrow penile urethra; emergency
- **Urolithiasis** in steers/wethers (struvite, calcium phosphate) — early castration → small urethral diameter
- **Urinary incontinence** in spayed female dogs — urethral sphincter mechanism incompetence (USMI)

## 10. Nervous System

### Central Nervous System (CNS) 🔴

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Brain anatomy](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=brain+anatomy&type=models) · [Dog brain](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=dog+brain&type=models) · [Brainstem & cerebellum](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=brainstem+cerebellum&type=models) · [Spinal cord](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=spinal+cord&type=models) (Sketchfab)

#### Brain Divisions
- **Cerebrum** (telencephalon): two hemispheres, gyri/sulci, frontal/parietal/temporal/occipital lobes
- **Diencephalon**: thalamus (relay), hypothalamus (homeostasis, endocrine), pituitary
- **Brainstem**:
  - **Midbrain (mesencephalon)**: CN III, IV; reflexes
  - **Pons**: bridges; CN V
  - **Medulla oblongata**: CN VIII–XII; respiratory/cardiac centers
- **Cerebellum**: coordination, balance — vermis + hemispheres + flocculonodular lobe

#### Meninges 🔴 (outer to inner)
1. **Dura mater** (tough)
2. **Arachnoid mater**
3. **Pia mater** (delicate, adheres to brain)

- **Subarachnoid space** contains **CSF** — this is where CSF is sampled (cisternal or lumbar tap)

#### Ventricles & CSF 🟡
- **Lateral ventricles → 3rd ventricle → cerebral aqueduct → 4th ventricle → central canal of spinal cord**
- CSF produced by **choroid plexus** (in ventricles)
- Reabsorbed by arachnoid villi → venous sinuses
- 🔴 **Hydrocephalus**: CSF accumulation → enlarged ventricles; common in toy breeds (Chihuahua), brachycephalic

### Spinal Cord 🔴

- Extends caudally from foramen magnum
- Ends at **conus medullaris** + **cauda equina** (nerves continuing caudally)
- 🟡 Termination level varies by species:
  - Dog: L6
  - Cat: L7
  - Horse: S2
  - Cow: S1
- 🟡 Cisternal puncture (cisterna magna) vs lumbar puncture (L5–L6 or L6–L7 in dog)

#### Spinal Cord Cross-Section 🔴
- **Gray matter (central, "H" shape)**: cell bodies; dorsal horn (sensory), ventral horn (motor), lateral horn (autonomic, T1–L3 sympathetic)
- **White matter (peripheral)**: axons in tracts
  - **Ascending** (sensory): spinothalamic, dorsal columns
  - **Descending** (motor): corticospinal, rubrospinal, reticulospinal

### Peripheral Nervous System 🟡

#### Brachial Plexus 🟡
- C6–T2 spinal nerves form plexus innervating forelimb
- Major branches: suprascapular, subscapular, axillary, musculocutaneous, **radial, median, ulnar**
- 🔴 **Radial nerve**: extensor of elbow + carpus; injury → "knuckling"
- 🔴 **Brachial plexus avulsion**: traumatic; loss of forelimb function

#### Lumbosacral Plexus 🟡
- L4–S2/3 → hindlimb innervation
- **Femoral n.**: extends stifle (quadriceps); injury → cannot bear weight
- **Sciatic n.**: largest nerve in body; flexion of stifle, extension of hock + foot
  - Branches: tibial (extends hock), peroneal (flexes hock, extends digits)
- 🔴 **Sciatic injury** in calves from prolonged dystocia or in cows from downer cow syndrome

### Special Senses 🟡

#### Eye 🔴

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Eye anatomy](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=eye+anatomy&type=models) · [Eyeball cross-section](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=eyeball+cross+section&type=models) · [Canine eye](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=canine+eye&type=models) (Sketchfab)

- **Outer fibrous tunic**: cornea (transparent) + sclera (white)
- **Middle vascular tunic (uvea)**: iris + ciliary body + choroid
- **Inner nervous tunic**: retina (rods + cones + ganglion cells → optic nerve)
- 🔴 **Tapetum lucidum**: reflective layer in choroid; enhances night vision; "eyeshine"
  - Present in dogs, cats, horses, ruminants — **NOT in pigs, primates, birds**
- **Aqueous humor** (anterior chamber) ← produced by ciliary body, drained at iridocorneal angle (glaucoma if blocked)
- **Vitreous humor** (posterior to lens; gel-like)
- 🟡 **Third eyelid (nictitating membrane)**: medial canthus; T-shaped cartilage; gland of third eyelid produces tear fluid; protrusion = dry eye, Horner's, dehydration, **Haw's syndrome (cats — bilateral protrusion + diarrhea)**
- 🔴 ⚡ **Cornea = 5 layers** (outside in): tear film, **epithelium** (heals fast — fluorescein UPTAKE if ulcerated), **stroma** (90% of thickness), **Descemet's membrane** (deep ulcer = "descemetocele" = surgical emergency, NO fluorescein uptake of Descemet's), **endothelium** (no regeneration in adults — corneal edema if damaged)
- 🟡 **Pecten** (birds): vascular pigmented structure projecting from optic disc into vitreous — supplies nutrients to avascular avian retina
- 🟡 **Photoreceptor distribution**: cone-rich = diurnal birds, primates (high color/acuity); rod-rich = nocturnal mammals (cats, dogs)

#### Ear 🟡

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Ear anatomy](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=ear+anatomy&type=models) · [Cochlea / inner ear](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=cochlea+inner+ear&type=models) · [Tympanic bulla](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=tympanic+bulla&type=models) (Sketchfab)

- **External ear**: pinna + external auditory canal (vertical + horizontal in dogs/cats)
- **Middle ear**: tympanic membrane + ossicles (malleus, incus, stapes) + tympanic bulla + Eustachian tube
  - 🟡 **Guttural pouches** in horses: ventral evaginations of Eustachian tube; site of empyema (Strep equi), mycosis (life-threatening epistaxis)
- **Inner ear**: cochlea (hearing) + vestibular system (semicircular canals, utricle, saccule — balance)

## 11. Endocrine System

### Pituitary Gland 🔴

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Pituitary gland](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=pituitary+gland&type=models) · [Hypothalamus](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=hypothalamus&type=models) (Sketchfab)

- 🔴 **Two functional parts**:
  - **Anterior (adenohypophysis)**: glandular; secretes **GH, ACTH, TSH, FSH, LH, prolactin** (mnemonic: "FLAT PiG" — FSH, LH, ACTH, TSH, Prolactin, iGH)
  - **Posterior (neurohypophysis)**: neural extension; releases **ADH and oxytocin** (synthesized in hypothalamus)
- 🔴 **Pars intermedia** (between anterior and posterior) — clinically important in horses:
  - **PPID (Equine Cushing's)** = pars intermedia dysfunction — old horses, hirsutism, laminitis

### Thyroid Gland 🟡
- Bilobed, lateral to trachea, just caudal to larynx; **isthmus** connecting lobes is variable in dogs, often absent in cats
- Follicles produce T3, T4 (require iodine)
- **C-cells (parafollicular)**: calcitonin (lowers blood Ca)
- 🔴 ⚡ **Cat thyroid 4-quadrant ectopic distribution**: ectopic functional thyroid tissue can be found from larynx to base of heart (mediastinum) → **failed I-131 or thyroidectomy if ectopic tissue not addressed**; pre-treatment scintigraphy helpful
- 🔴 **Goiter**: enlarged thyroid; iodine deficiency, congenital, neoplasia
- 🔴 ⚡ **Feline hyperthyroidism**: **#1 endocrinopathy in older cats**; usually multinodular hyperplasia (benign adenomatous, ~98%); palpable cervical "thyroid slip"; weight loss + polyphagia + tachycardia + hypertension

### Parathyroid Glands 🟡
- 4 small glands, embedded in/near thyroid
- Secrete **PTH** (parathyroid hormone): increases blood Ca by:
  - Mobilizing bone Ca
  - Increasing renal Ca reabsorption
  - Activating vitamin D → ↑ intestinal Ca absorption
- 🔴 **Primary hyperparathyroidism** (rare): adenoma → hypercalcemia
- 🔴 **Renal secondary hyperparathyroidism**: CKD → P retention → ↓Ca → PTH stimulated chronically → "rubber jaw" in young animals

### Adrenal Glands 🔴

🔗 **Interactive 3D**: [Adrenal gland anatomy](https://sketchfab.com/search?q=adrenal+gland&type=models) (Sketchfab)

- 🟡 **Anatomic position**: rostromedial to each kidney, retroperitoneal
- 🔴 ⚡ **Right adrenal lies adjacent to the caudal vena cava** — surgical hazard during right adrenalectomy (tumor invasion of CVC = poor prognosis); left adrenalectomy easier
- **Cortex** (3 zones — mnemonic "GFR makes good **s**alt, **s**ugar, **s**ex"):
  - **Zona Glomerulosa**: aldosterone (mineralocorticoid — Na/K balance)
  - **Zona Fasciculata**: cortisol (glucocorticoid — stress, gluconeogenesis)
  - **Zona Reticularis**: sex steroids (androgens, estrogens — small amounts)
- **Medulla**: catecholamines (epinephrine, norepinephrine) — chromaffin cells
- 🔴 ⚡ **Hyperadrenocorticism (HAC, Cushing's)**: PDH (pituitary, 85%) vs adrenal-dependent (15%); dogs >> cats; pot-belly, PU/PD, alopecia, calcinosis cutis
- 🔴 ⚡ **Hypoadrenocorticism (Addison's)**: immune-mediated cortex destruction; "**great pretender**"; **NSDTR + Standard Poodle predisposed**; **hyperK + hypoNa, Na:K <27**, ↓ cortisol on ACTH stim
- 🔴 **Pheochromocytoma**: adrenal medulla tumor; episodic hypertension, paroxysmal collapse, panting

### Pancreas (Endocrine) 🔴
- **Islets of Langerhans** (1–2% of pancreas)
- **α-cells** (~20%): glucagon — raises blood glucose
- **β-cells** (~70%): insulin — lowers blood glucose
- **δ-cells** (~5%): somatostatin — inhibits GH, insulin, glucagon
- **F/PP cells**: pancreatic polypeptide
- 🔴 **Diabetes mellitus**: 
  - Dogs: predominantly Type 1 (β-cell destruction)
  - Cats: predominantly Type 2 (insulin resistance + amyloid deposition)

### Other Endocrine Tissues 🟢
- **Pineal gland**: melatonin — circadian rhythm, photoperiod (seasonal breeding)
- **Thymus**: T-cell maturation (involutes after puberty)
- **Heart atria**: ANP (atrial natriuretic peptide) — natriuresis
- **Kidney**: erythropoietin (EPO), renin, calcitriol (active vitamin D)
- **GI tract**: gastrin, secretin, CCK, ghrelin

## 12. Integumentary System (Skin)

### Skin Layers 🟡
- **Epidermis** (avascular): stratum basale → spinosum → granulosum → lucidum (only in thick skin) → corneum (keratin)
- **Dermis**: collagen, elastin, blood vessels, nerves, hair follicles, glands
- **Hypodermis (subcutis)**: fat, loose CT

### Hair Follicles 🟡
- **Compound (in dogs/cats)**: multiple hair shafts per follicle (1 primary + multiple secondary hairs)
- **Simple (in horses, cattle)**: one hair per follicle
- **Hair growth cycle**: anagen (growth) → catagen (transition) → telogen (resting)
- 🟡 **Telogen effluvium**: shedding after stress/illness/parturition

### Glands 🟡
| Gland | Location | Secretion | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sebaceous** | Associated with hair follicles | Sebum (oily) | Skin lubrication |
| **Apocrine sweat** | Most of body in dog/cat | Watery, with proteins | Pheromone signaling; minimal cooling |
| **Eccrine sweat** | Footpads (dogs/cats), entire body in horses/humans | Watery, electrolytes | Cooling |
| **Anal sacs** | Lateral to anus | Foul-smelling fluid | Communication; impaction common in dogs |
| **Tail glands** | Dorsal tail base | "Stud tail" in cats | |
| **Mammary** | Ventral abdomen | Milk | Modified apocrine |

🔴 **Dogs/cats sweat through footpads only** — primary thermoregulation = panting
🔴 **Horses sweat profusely** all over body — important during exercise

### Specialized Structures 🟡
- **Hooves (horse)**: wall, sole, frog, white line — keratin from coronary band
- **Claws (dog/cat)**: P3 + horny sheath; cat claws retractable (deep digital flexor tendon)
- **Horns (cattle/sheep/goat)**: **cornual process of frontal bone** + horn sheath (keratin); horn cavity is continuous with frontal sinus (sinusitis after dehorning); ⚡ **cornual nerve block (branch of CN V — zygomaticotemporal)** for dehorning
- **Antlers (deer)**: shed annually; bone covered in velvet (skin) during growth
- **Foot pads (dog/cat)**: thick keratinized epidermis; metacarpal/metatarsal pad + digital pads + carpal pad
- 🟡 **Feather types** (birds):
  - **Contour feathers** = body covering, vaned, includes flight feathers (remiges) + tail (rectrices)
  - **Down feathers** = soft, no vane; insulation
  - **Filoplume feathers** = sensory hairlike feathers
  - **Bristle feathers** = around eyes, beak (sensory)
  - **Powder down** = continuously growing, breaks into "powder" (parrots — health indicator)
- 🟢 **Avian beak (rhamphotheca)** = keratinized covering of mandible/maxilla; continuously grows (~4 mm/month in raptors); overgrowth from liver disease, malocclusion

## 13. Embryology Quick Reference

### Germ Layers 🟡
- **Ectoderm** → epidermis, nervous system, neural crest derivatives (PNS, melanocytes, adrenal medulla, dental enamel)
- **Mesoderm** → muscle, bone, cartilage, blood, kidneys, gonads, dermis, heart
- **Endoderm** → GI lining, respiratory lining, liver, pancreas, thyroid, bladder

### Common Congenital Defects 🔴
- **PDA**: failure of ductus arteriosus closure → ⚡ continuous machinery murmur, female small breeds
- **Persistent right aortic arch (PRAA)**: vascular ring → ⚡ regurgitation at weaning
- **VSD/ASD**: septal defects (VSD usually subaortic in dogs)
- **Subaortic stenosis (SAS)** — ⚡ Newfoundland, Boxer, Golden — syncope, sudden death
- **Pulmonic stenosis** — Bulldog, Boxer
- **Cleft palate**: failure of palatal shelves to fuse (brachycephalics, ergot/teratogen exposure)
- **Cryptorchidism**: failure of testicular descent → Sertoli risk
- **Atresia ani**: failure of anal canalization
- **Patent urachus** (foal — ⚡ urine dripping from umbilicus)
- **Spina bifida**: failure of neural tube closure (⚡ **Manx cats predisposed** — sacrocaudal dysgenesis)

### Fetal Membranes 🟡
- **Amnion** (innermost): bathes fetus in amniotic fluid
- **Allantois**: waste sac, derived from hindgut → forms the **urachus** (allantoic duct from bladder to umbilicus)
- **Chorion** (outermost): contacts maternal uterus, forms placental interface
- **Yolk sac**: regresses early in domestic mammals (large in birds/reptiles)

### Twin / Multiple Pregnancies 🔴
- 🔴 ⚡ **Freemartin (cow)**: female calf born co-twin to a male — **~90% sterile** — placental anastomoses share blood → **AMH (anti-Müllerian hormone) from male twin** suppresses female reproductive tract development; also **XX/XY blood chimerism**
  - Diagnose: vaginal length <10 cm in newborn heifer (vs 13-15 cm normal), or karyotype showing chimerism
  - Tip: **only ~10% of bull-heifer twin sets produce a fertile heifer** — usually cull
- 🟡 Sheep/goat freemartins exist but rare (different placental structure)
- 🟡 **Equine twin pregnancies** = obstetric emergency — usually one or both lost; manual reduction at day 14-16 standard practice

### Placentation 🔴
| Type | Species | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Diffuse epitheliochorial** | Mare, sow | Microcotyledons spread over chorion; minimal IgG transfer in utero (foal/piglet relies entirely on colostrum) |
| **Cotyledonary syndesmochorial** | Cow, sheep, goat | Caruncle (maternal) + cotyledon (fetal) = placentome; some IgG transfer in utero |
| **Zonary endotheliochorial** | Dog, cat | Band-shaped; intermediate IgG transfer |
| **Discoid hemochorial** | Primates, rodents | Disc-shaped; placental hormones extensive; high IgG transfer |

🔴 **Clinical relevance**: 
- **Foals/calves are born essentially agammaglobulinemic** — colostrum critical (failure of passive transfer = FPT)
- **Cats/dogs**: more in utero IgG transfer but colostrum still important

## 14. Histology — High-Yield Microanatomy 🔴

### Epithelial Tissue 🔴
| Type | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple squamous** | Alveoli, Bowman's capsule, endothelium | Diffusion |
| **Simple cuboidal** | Renal tubules, glands | Secretion/absorption |
| **Simple columnar** | GI tract (most), gallbladder | Absorption/secretion |
| **Pseudostratified ciliated columnar** | Trachea, large airways, vas deferens | Mucociliary clearance |
| **Stratified squamous (keratinized)** | Skin, gingiva, hard palate | Protection from abrasion |
| **Stratified squamous (non-keratinized)** | Esophagus, vagina, oral cavity | Protection (moist) |
| 🔴 ⚡ **Transitional (urothelium)** | **Urinary tract** (renal pelvis → bladder → proximal urethra) | Stretch — appears cuboidal when relaxed, squamous when stretched |

### Connective Tissue 🟡
- **Loose (areolar)**: lamina propria, hypodermis — fibroblasts in matrix
- **Dense regular**: tendons, ligaments — parallel collagen
- **Dense irregular**: dermis, organ capsules — woven collagen
- **Cartilage** (3 types):
  - 🔴 **Hyaline** (most common — articular, tracheal rings, growth plates) — type II collagen
  - **Elastic** (epiglottis, pinna) — elastic fibers
  - **Fibrocartilage** (intervertebral discs, menisci, pubic symphysis) — type I collagen, tensile strength
- **Bone**:
  - **Woven** = immature, post-fracture callus, fetal — random collagen
  - **Lamellar** = mature, organized — compact (osteons/Haversian) + spongy (trabeculae)
- **Adipose** = energy storage, insulation, endocrine (leptin)
- **Blood** = considered specialized CT (RBCs, WBCs, platelets in plasma)

### Muscle Tissue 🔴
| Type | Nuclei | Striations | Junctions | Regeneration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Skeletal** | Multinucleate, peripheral | Yes | None | Yes (satellite cells) |
| 🔴 **Cardiac** | 1-2, central | Yes | **Intercalated discs** (gap junctions, desmosomes) | **NO — fibrous scar** |
| 🔴 **Smooth** | 1, central | No | Gap junctions in unitary smooth | Yes |

### Nervous Tissue 🔴
- **Neurons**:
  - **Multipolar** (most CNS, motor) — many dendrites
  - **Bipolar** (retina, olfactory, vestibular) — one dendrite + one axon
  - **Pseudounipolar** (dorsal root ganglia — sensory) — single process splits
- **Glial cells** (mnemonic — A SOME):
  - 🔴 **Astrocytes** = blood-brain barrier, K⁺ buffering, scar formation
  - 🔴 **Oligodendrocytes** (CNS) / **Schwann cells** (PNS) = myelination
  - **Microglia** = CNS macrophages (only mesoderm-derived glial cell)
  - **Ependymal cells** = line ventricles, produce/circulate CSF (with choroid plexus)

### Organ-Specific Histology 🔴

#### Kidney 🔴
- **Glomerulus**: capillary tuft within Bowman's capsule
  - **Podocytes** (visceral epithelium) — interdigitating foot processes form filtration slits
  - **Mesangial cells** — supportive cells; structural + immune (proliferate in glomerulonephritis)
  - **3-layer filtration barrier**: fenestrated endothelium → basement membrane → podocyte slit diaphragms
- **PCT** = brush border (microvilli — increase reabsorption surface), eosinophilic cytoplasm; reabsorbs glucose, AA, ~65% Na/H2O
- **Loop of Henle** thin segments = simple squamous; thick ascending = cuboidal (concentrating mechanism)
- **DCT** = no brush border; site of aldosterone, PTH action
- **Collecting duct** = ADH-responsive (aquaporin-2 insertion)

#### Liver 🔴
- 🔴 ⚡ **Hepatic lobule (classical model)**: hexagonal, **central vein** in middle, **portal triads** (hepatic artery + portal vein + bile duct) at corners
- **Sinusoids** = fenestrated capillaries between hepatocyte plates; lined by **Kupffer cells** (liver macrophages)
- 🔴 ⚡ **Hepatic zones**:
  - **Zone 1 (periportal)** = closest to portal triad, most O2/nutrients first; **most affected by viral hepatitis, toxins entering via portal vein**
  - **Zone 2** = intermediate
  - **Zone 3 (centrilobular, around central vein)** = lowest O2 → **most susceptible to hypoxia, ischemia, and oxidative toxins (acetaminophen, CCl4, halothane)**
- **Space of Disse** = perisinusoidal space (Ito/stellate cells store vitamin A; activated → fibrosis in cirrhosis)

#### Spleen 🟡
- 🔴 **White pulp** = lymphoid: **PALS (periarteriolar lymphoid sheath)** = T-cells around central artery; **B-cell follicles** off PALS
- 🔴 **Red pulp** = sinusoids + cords of Billroth; filters senescent RBCs (macrophages)
- **Marginal zone** between red and white = antigen sampling

#### Lymph Node 🟡
- **Cortex** = B-cell follicles (germinal centers when active)
- **Paracortex** = T-cell zone
- **Medulla** = medullary cords + sinuses (plasma cells, macrophages)

#### Intestine 🔴
- **Villi + microvilli (brush border)** = massively increase absorptive surface
- **Crypts of Lieberkühn** = epithelial stem cells, secretion (Paneth cells = antimicrobial defensins; goblet cells = mucus)
- 🔴 ⚡ **Peyer's patches (ileum)** = aggregated lymphoid follicles; **M cells** in overlying epithelium = antigen sampling for immune system
  - 🔴 ⚡ **Cat alimentary lymphoma**: **#1 site = small intestine**, often ileum (Peyer's patch origin); FeLV-positive cats predisposed historically

#### Lung 🔴
- **Conducting zone** (no gas exchange): trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → terminal bronchioles
- **Respiratory zone**: respiratory bronchioles → alveolar ducts → alveoli
- 🔴 ⚡ **Alveolar cells**:
  - **Type I pneumocytes** (squamous) = 95% of alveolar surface; gas exchange
  - **Type II pneumocytes** (cuboidal) = produce **surfactant** (DPPC — reduces surface tension, prevents collapse); also stem cells for repair
  - **Alveolar macrophages** = clear debris, surfactant turnover
- 🟡 Surfactant deficiency in premature foals/lambs → respiratory distress syndrome

#### Pancreas 🟢
- **Exocrine acini** (~98%) = serous; secrete digestive enzymes into ducts
- **Islets of Langerhans** (~2%) = scattered endocrine clusters: α (glucagon), β (insulin, central), δ (somatostatin), F/PP
